Build enough HPC awareness to understand the field, recognize opportunities, and know where to go for deeper technical learning.

What is HPC? High-performance computing (HPC) uses clusters of powerful, tightly connected computers to solve problems that are too large or too complex for a single machine, running calculations in parallel across many processors at once.

What is a supercomputer? A supercomputer is a large-scale computing system built from many interconnected processors working together to solve problems far beyond the capacity of a single computer.

What kinds of research use HPC? HPC supports research across nearly every discipline, including climate modeling, genomics, materials science, engineering, and social science simulations.

NAIRR Pilot: The National AI Research Resource (NAIRR) Pilot is a federally led initiative that connects U.S. researchers and educators to the computational, data, and training resources needed to advance AI research. It is coordinated by the National Science Foundation along with other federal agencies and private-sector partners, and it has supported hundreds of research projects nationwide.

These resources are provided as places to learn more; the Campus Champion roadmap does not attempt to reproduce the technical training offered by other HPC organizations.

Programming and parallel computing: Parallel programming techniques like MPI and OpenMP let your code split work across many processors at once. This is essential for running efficiently on HPC systems.

Advanced HPC topics: Once you're comfortable with the basics, advanced topics include GPU computing, large-scale machine learning, and performance optimization. Check with your local HPC center for specialized training.

Bring HPC awareness back to your campus and help other people discover opportunities in advanced computing.

Share an HPC resource: Found a useful HPC tool, tutorial, or article? Share it with the Champions community through your usual communication channels.

Share a research example: Highlight a research project that used HPC and share it with the Champions community.

Talk about HPC with students and colleagues: Use what you've learned to introduce HPC concepts informally to students and colleagues on your campus. Even a short conversation can spark interest.

Tell your Campus Champion story: Share your experience as a Champion with the broader community.

Introduce HPC to a class or student group: Offer to give a short talk or demo introducing HPC to a class, club, or student group on your campus.

Host or support an informational activity: Organize or help run an event, such as a workshop, info session, or lab tour, to raise HPC awareness on campus.

Connect researchers with computing resources: Help researchers on your campus identify and request the right HPC allocations for their work.

Share opportunities with your campus community: Pass along HPC-related opportunities, such as training, allocations, and events, to researchers and students on your campus.
